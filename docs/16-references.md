# References and Method Notes

The artifacts in this repository are lightweight educational adaptations of established project-management practices. They are not PMI templates or a claim of PMI endorsement.

## Public references

- Project Management Institute (PMI), [What is Project Management?](https://www.pmi.org/es-419/about/what-is-project-management) — general definition and purpose of project management.
- PMI, [Project Charter: What It Is and How to Write One](https://www.pmi.org/blog/project-charter) — business need, objectives, scope, stakeholders, assumptions, constraints, and authorization.
- PMI, [Project Management Basics](https://www.pmi.org/help-marketplace/~/link.aspx?_id=FF99743C71A271367017995CAEBDD95A&_z=z) — introductory coverage of integration, scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, communications, risk, procurement, and stakeholders.
- PMI, [Getting Back to Basics](https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/project-management-getting-back-basics-9313) — charter, requirements, WBS, and baseline concepts.

## Method notes

- The project charter defines the purpose, objectives, high-level scope, constraints, assumptions, risks, and success criteria.
- The WBS is deliverable-oriented and decomposes the scope into manageable work packages.
- The schedule uses relative estimates and explicit dependencies; it does not claim actual historical dates.
- RACI clarifies responsibility and accountability. In this individual case study, role labels describe responsibilities performed by one person.
- Risk ratings are qualitative planning assessments, not statistically calibrated probabilities.
- Agile iterations are proposed to explain an iterative delivery approach; historical Scrum events and velocity were not recorded.
- KPI definitions are provided for future measurement, but schedule/cost performance indices are omitted because valid baseline and actual data are unavailable.
